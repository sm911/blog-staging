Identity Is the New Perimeter: Five Owner Questions After CISA/NIST IR 8587

On September 15, 2026, CISA and NIST told federal agencies and cloud providers to harden the tokens behind SSO. Here is what SMB owners should ask this month — in plain English.

Jim Venuto · September 22, 2026 · 8 min read

Most small and mid-size businesses I advise do not think they have a “federal cloud identity” problem. They have Microsoft 365. Or Google Workspace. Or a customer portal with “Sign in with…” and an MSP who swears MFA is on.

That is exactly why Interagency Report (IR) 8587 matters outside the Beltway. On September 15, 2026, CISA and NIST published final guidance on protecting tokens and assertions — the short-lived credentials behind single sign-on (SSO), federation, and API access — from forgery, theft, and misuse. Adversaries increasingly steal or forge those tokens to move laterally and reach sensitive data after the password prompt looks fine.

CISA’s Chris Butera put it plainly: identity is the new perimeter. You do not need a Security Decision Record committee to use the idea. You need five owner questions and answers in writing.

What IR 8587 Actually Covers

The final report builds on NIST SP 800-53 (Release 5.1.1) and control IA-13. It highlights architectural considerations for identity providers and authorization servers; better key management, token verification, and token life-cycle controls; guidance for securing SSO, federation, and API access that rely on digitally signed tokens; and principles for configurable, transparent, interoperable controls.

It is written for agencies and cloud service providers. Cloud consumers — that is you — are explicitly urged to review and implement it. Nearly 250 public comments shaped the final, and major IdP and cloud vendors participated in the feedback loop.

Five Questions for Owners and Boards

1. Where do our SSO tokens live, and who can mint them?

Ask for a one-page map: primary IdP (Entra ID, Okta, Google, etc.), apps that trust it, and any “shadow” SSO (legacy VPN portals, niche SaaS with their own identity). If the answer is “the MSP knows,” that is not an answer — that is a dependency.

2. How long can a stolen session stay alive?

Session lifetime, refresh-token policy, and “keep me signed in” defaults decide how useful a stolen browser cookie or device token is. Ask for current settings on email, finance, EHR/portal, and remote access — and whether high-risk apps force re-auth.

3. Is MFA phishing-resistant where it matters?

OTP texts and push fatigue are better than nothing and weaker than FIDO2/passkeys or certificate-based options for admins and finance. Ask which roles still use SMS OTP, and the date you will move break-glass and privileged roles to phishing-resistant methods.

4. Can we see token abuse in the logs we already pay for?

IR 8587 stresses detection at scale. You need: IdP sign-in logs retained, alerts for impossible travel / token replay patterns your vendor supports, and a named person who opens those alerts. “We have Microsoft 365” is not the same as “someone reviews Entra sign-in risk weekly.”

5. What did our critical SaaS vendors say about token hardening?

Send a short vendor question: Do you support shorter-lived access tokens, continuous access evaluation / session revocation, and documented guidance aligned with IR 8587 / modern token hygiene? File the answers next to your cyber-insurance application.

What “Good Enough” Looks Like This Month

  • One page: IdP + top 10 apps that trust it.
  • Privileged roles on phishing-resistant MFA (or a dated plan).
  • Session/lifetime settings documented for email and finance.
  • Weekly 15-minute review of IdP risk or unfamiliar sign-ins — on the calendar, not the wish list.
  • Break-glass accounts inventoried, monitored, and rarely used.

This is leadership work, not a new product purchase. The federal report gives you permission to ask harder questions of the people who already bill you for “identity.”
