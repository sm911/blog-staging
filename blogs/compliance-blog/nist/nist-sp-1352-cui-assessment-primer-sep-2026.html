Before the Customer Audit: NIST’s Small Business Primer for Assessing CUI Controls (SP 1352)

A September 16, 2026 NIST guide for small businesses that must assess SP 800-171Ar3 — useful whether your customer is a prime contractor, a manufacturer, or any buyer who asks about Controlled Unclassified Information.

By James Venuto | September 23, 2026 | Security Medic Consulting, LLC - AI Cyber Security and Privacy

If a big customer just emailed you a security questionnaire — or mentioned “CUI,” “800-171,” or “we need evidence before renewal” — you are not alone. Small and mid-size businesses across professional services, manufacturing, healthcare vendors, and IT providers increasingly sit in someone else’s regulated supply chain even when they never bid on a federal contract themselves.

On September 16, 2026, NIST published Special Publication SP 1352, Assessing Security Requirements for Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI): NIST SP 800-171Ar3 Small Business Primer, authored by Daniel Eliot and Victoria Pillitteri. The primer gives owners and operators a high-level overview of SP 800-171A Revision 3 — the assessment companion to the SP 800-171r3 protection requirements — plus basic strategies for planning an assessment.

I work with Hudson Valley and regional SMBs who feel assessment language before they feel ready. SP 1352 will not do the assessment for you. It does something more useful first: it lowers the intimidation tax so you can plan the work instead of freezing.

What SP 1352 Is (and Is Not)

What it is: A small-business primer for assessing security requirements for CUI under SP 800-171Ar3 — foundational assessment concepts and planning strategies for leaders and staff who manage SP 800-171r3, including self-assessments or prep for external assessors.
What it is not: A replacement for SP 800-171r3 itself, or for NIST’s earlier small-business implementation primer (SP 1318) on protecting CUI. Implementation and assessment are related; they are not the same document.

NIST’s CSRC announcement is explicit about audience: business leaders or employees tasked with managing SP 800-171r3 implementation, including self-assessments or preparing to work with external assessors. That includes plenty of firms that never wear a “defense contractor” label — anyone handling customer CUI or answering supply-chain security asks.

Why Every SMB in a Regulated Supply Chain Should Care

  • Customer contracts travel. Flow-downs, BAAs, and vendor security addenda often import assessment expectations even when you are two layers away from the original buyer.
  • Assessment is a project, not a PDF. Knowing how evidence will be examined changes how you store screenshots, tickets, and policies before the deadline.
  • Self-assessment still needs a method. Guessing “implemented” without a procedure is how questionnaires fail underwriter and customer review alike.
  • Limited staff is the design constraint. NIST wrote SP 1352 because small businesses juggle time, budget, and thin IT benches — the primer meets you there.

A Practical Planning Sequence I Recommend

  1. Confirm whether CUI (or equivalent customer-sensitive data) is actually in scope. If the customer cannot name the data types, ask them to. Scope fights are cheaper before the assessment calendar starts.
  2. Separate “protect” from “assess.” Use SP 800-171r3 / SP 1318 themes for building controls; use SP 800-171Ar3 / SP 1352 themes for how you will prove them.
  3. Name an evidence owner per family of requirements. “IT will handle it” is not a name. Pick people for access control evidence, logging evidence, and media/disposal evidence.
  4. Decide self-assessment vs. third-party help early. SP 1352 is written for both paths. Budget and calendar change if an external assessor is coming.
  5. Build a living evidence folder now. Policy PDFs, config exports, ticket samples, and training attestations with dates beat a scramble the week of the customer visit.
This week’s action: Download SP 1352 from NIST. Skim the planning sections. Write a one-page scope note: which systems might process customer CUI, who owns evidence, and the next customer deadline. Put the dated note in your compliance folder.

If an MSP Runs Your Stack

Many owners I meet already have a managed service provider. Fine. Ask them in one sentence: “Please confirm which of our systems could process customer Controlled Unclassified Information or equivalent restricted data, what evidence we already have for an SP 800-171-style assessment, and what is still missing before our next customer security review.” You want a written answer — not a hallway shrug.
