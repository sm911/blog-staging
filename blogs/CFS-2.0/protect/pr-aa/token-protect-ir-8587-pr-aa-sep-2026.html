Protect PR.AA the Token Way: Mapping CISA/NIST IR 8587 to NIST CSF 2.0 for SMBs

Passwords got the MFA campaign. Tokens steal the session afterward. Here is how to put IR 8587 into Protect — Identity Management, Authentication, and Access Control.

Jim Venuto September 22, 2026 10 min read

Key Takeaways

  • CISA and NIST released IR 8587 on September 15, 2026 to harden SSO tokens, assertions, key management, and token lifecycles.
  • NIST CSF 2.0 Protect → PR.AA is the natural home for those outcomes in an SMB program.
  • Staff four workstreams: issuance trust, verification, lifecycle/revocation, and detection — not a new IdP project by default.
  • Pair with Govern (policies) and Detect (IdP logging) so Protect is not a checkbox.

Yesterday’s cyber program often stopped at “MFA is on.” Attackers noticed. Session cookies, OAuth tokens, and forged assertions let them walk past the password prompt you celebrated last year.

On September 15, 2026, CISA and NIST published Interagency Report IR 8587, Protecting Tokens and Assertions from Forgery, Theft, and Misuse. It guides agencies and cloud service providers on architectural choices for identity providers and authorization servers; enhancements to key management, token verification, and life-cycle controls; and principles for securing SSO, federation, and API access that rely on digitally signed tokens. CISA urges federal agencies, CSPs, and cloud consumers to review and implement it.

If you run your cybersecurity program on NIST CSF 2.0, you do not need a new framework. You need a clearer Protect outcome.

Where It Lands in CSF 2.0

Protect (PR) — safeguards to ensure delivery of services. Inside Protect, PR.AA — Identity Management, Authentication, and Access Control covers proving identity, managing credentials and authenticators, and controlling access. Token theft sits squarely here: authentication succeeded once; authorization kept succeeding for hours because nobody treated the token as a first-class credential.

Related CSF pieces you should wire in without boiling the ocean:

  • Govern (GV.RM / GV.OC) — policy that tokens and sessions are in scope for risk decisions and vendor due diligence.
  • Identify (ID.AM) — inventory IdPs and apps that mint or consume tokens.
  • Detect (DE.CM) — continuous monitoring of sign-in and token anomalies.

Four PR.AA Workstreams SMBs Can Staff

1. Issuance trust

Who is allowed to mint tokens that your apps trust? Document the primary IdP, any secondary “Sign in with…” paths, and apps that still use long-lived API keys instead of short-lived tokens. Prefer asymmetric signing and vendor-documented key rotation.

2. Verification

Apps should validate issuer, audience, signature, and expiry — not just “a JWT appeared.” For customer portals and internal APIs, ask developers or vendors whether token validation follows current library guidance (and whether they pin trusted issuers).

3. Lifecycle and revocation

How fast can you kill a session when HR terminates access or a laptop is stolen? Map refresh-token lifetimes, continuous access evaluation / conditional access, and break-glass procedures. IR 8587’s life-cycle theme is useless if your “disable user” ticket takes three days to reach the IdP.

4. Detection

Retain IdP logs. Alert on unfamiliar locations, impossible travel (when the product supports it), sudden consent grants, and mass token refresh. Assign a human — even 30 minutes a week — to open the queue.

This week’s PR.AA drill: (1) List your IdP and top 10 apps. (2) Record session/refresh settings for email and finance. (3) Confirm privileged roles use phishing-resistant MFA or have a dated plan. (4) Put a recurring calendar block titled “IdP risk review.”
